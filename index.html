The Mechanism

How a supervisor falls

Virginia law gives citizens the power to remove elected officials who stop listening. This board is how we hold them accountable.

01 / GROUNDS

State the cause

The petition names the neglect of duty, misuse of office, or incompetence — in writing, with reasonable accuracy and detail.

02 / PLEDGE

Build the list

Citizens pledge here. We watch each district climb toward its 10% threshold. Every name is a number a supervisor can see rising.

03 / THRESHOLD

The bar fills

When a district hits its number, we email every pledge holder with signing locations and dates. Registered voters sign by hand — the only signature that counts.

04 / LEVERAGE

They listen — or we file

A board that sees the numbers climbing has a choice: represent the people, or face a petition in Circuit Court. The point is to make them govern like they're being watched — because they are.

§ 24.2-233

The grounds are already here

A supervisor doesn't need to be convicted of a crime. The law reaches conduct this board has watched unfold.

  • Neglect of duty — failing to do the job the voters elected them to do.
  • Misuse of office — failing to disclose a conflict of interest, or refusing to recuse from data-center and transmission-line votes where they stand to benefit, under the State and Local Government Conflict of Interests Act.
  • Incompetence in the performance of official duties.
  • A violation rising to a Class 1 misdemeanor.

This applies to all of them

The conflict-of-interest grounds do not care how long someone has held the seat. A supervisor sworn in last month is just as accountable as one serving for years. Secret deals, family land sold for data centers, undisclosed interests, votes that should have been recused — every one is a documented path to removal.
