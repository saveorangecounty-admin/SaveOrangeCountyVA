How it works

From a pledge to a filing

A pledge here is a headcount, not a signature. The signature that counts is collected in person, on paper, later. Here is the whole sequence.

  1. 1

    You pledge

    Name, email, district. It tells us how many people in your district are ready when the time comes.

  2. 2

    A district reaches its number

    Each district needs signatures equal to 10% of the votes cast in that supervisor's last election.

  3. 3

    We hold a signing

    We email everyone who pledged with dates and locations. Registered voters sign by hand, on the state form, under penalty of perjury. This is the only signature with legal effect.

  4. 4

    The petition is filed

    Signatures go to the Circuit Court. The court — not the petitioners — decides removal, on clear and convincing evidence.

The grounds. Va. Code § 24.2-233 permits removal for neglect of a clear duty of the office, misuse of the office, or incompetence in performing it — in each case where the conduct has had a material adverse effect on the conduct of the office. No criminal conviction is required. The specific grounds asserted in any filed petition are the responsibility of the petitioners, and an attorney should review them before filing.

The Issues

What's on the table

Transmission

Joshua Falls–Yeat, 765kV

Valley Link — a joint venture of Dominion Energy, Transource and FirstEnergy — proposes about 115 miles of 765kV line from Campbell County to Culpeper County, crossing up to nine counties including Orange. Structures run roughly 135–160 feet on a 200-foot right-of-way. Approval rests with the State Corporation Commission, not the county.

Zoning

The Technology District

Adopted December 2025. It is a floating district — it designates no specific sites, sets no cap on total acreage, and places no limit on how many parcels countywide may be rezoned for data centers. In January 2026 the Board initiated ZTA 26-01 to remove data centers as a by-right use, routing future proposals through special use permits.
